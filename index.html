A resignation is a $27,217 invoice nobody budgeted

A fully loaded senior engineer costs $18,290 a month, $105.52 an hour, and the offer letter is the cheapest document in the stack. Recruiting runs near $13,500 before day one, and the first sixty days are ramp instead of output: 50 percent speed in month one, 75 percent in month two, full salary the entire time.

Run the ledger on one hire. The first month pays $18,290 for half speed output and burns $9,145. The second month pays full salary for 75 percent output and burns $4,572.50. Ramp dead spend: $13,717.50. Add the recruiting bill and the seat has cost $27,217.50 before its first fully productive week, none of it visible in any budget line named engineering.

Turnovers replay the invoice. Say the hire lasts two years and takes an offer elsewhere; the seat refills and every line comes back: recruiting, ramp, and the weeks the replacement spends relearning a codebase the predecessor knew cold. Two cycles across four years puts the dead spend at $54,435, which is 9.08 subscription months at $5,995 burned on the chair instead of the work.

The subscription carries none of these lines. There is no requisition, no ramp curve, and no exit interview; the board keeps every delivered card and its history, so the next card starts at full speed the moment it is written. Price a salary against $5,995 and the honest comparison adds the dead months the seat quietly accumulates on its own schedule.
